## 1. The death of per-seat pricing  **[strongest available]**

**Narrative.** February 2026's software selloff, "Black February", wiped more than $1T of
market cap; the per-seat model is being repriced because an agent does the work of ten people
without asking for ten seats. Gartner: by 2030 at least 40% of enterprise SaaS spend shifts to
usage, agent or outcome pricing, and seat-based vendor revenue share falls from 21% to 15%.

**Freshness.** February 2026 event, still the dominant frame in software discourse and being
re-litigated every earnings cycle. Cite as a repricing already underway, not as news.

**ICP survival.** Excellent, and rare. This is one of the few big narratives that is *more*
true at 2 to 50 people than at enterprise: a founder with six staff feels a per-seat bill for
software that an agent now operates, immediately and personally.

**Ultron mapping.** Direct and structural, not rhetorical. The AI work is metered as
pay-as-you-go credits through OpenRouter, not per human. Seven agents cover the jobs a GTM
team would have needed seats for.

> **Honesty flag, must be handled in the script.** Ultron Enterprise is **$297/mo per user**,
> which *is* per-seat. A film that says "per-seat is dead" while the pricing page sells seats
> is a gift to anyone in the comments. The defensible and more interesting version: the seat
> pays for the human's access, the **credits pay for the labour**, and the two have been
> conflated for fifteen years. Do not run this narrative without that distinction in beat 5.

**Proposed hook (verdict style, section 22).** `Your software still charges|for *people*.|The work|stopped being *people*.`
**Keyword.** SEATS.
**Collision.** Clean. 03 was cost *per token between models*; this is cost *per unit of who*.

## What the "big creator" search is actually worth

Searching for what large AI creators post returns mostly tool-affiliate SEO. The signal is not
in their formats, it is in **which underlying research they choose to amplify**, and that is
findable directly. The working method, which produced episode 04:

1. Find the *primary* (a research firm, an earnings call, a filing), not the creator's post.
2. Date the primary. Most viral AI stats are re-circulating material a year or more old, and
   citing one as new is the cheapest possible way to lose credibility in the comments.
3. Check it survives the ICP. Enterprise numbers about 33 pilots do not describe an
   eight-person company, and pretending otherwise is the tell that the post was assembled
   rather than lived.
4. Check the Ultron answer is a **feature that exists and has been filmed**, not a claim.
5. Check the subject against the burnt list above.

An entry that fails 3 or 4 makes a decent caption and a bad film.

## 6. Your agent now has to say it is an agent  **[strongest available, and the freshest]**

**Narrative.** The EU AI Act's Article 50 transparency obligations went live on **2 August 2026**
- three days before this entry was written. An AI system that interacts with a person must be
built so the person knows they are dealing with a machine, and the Commission's own guidance
extends that to the agent identifying **who it represents**. Penalties reach **EUR 15M or 3% of
worldwide turnover**. The line that matters for a founder: the obligation lands on **deployers,
not only providers, even when the tools belong to third parties**. You are the deployer of the
AI that sends your outbound.

The United States half is already enforceable and is the part with teeth for a small company:
**California SB 243** has been fully in force since 1 January 2026 and carries a **private right
of action at $1,000 per violation** plus attorney fees. Oregon SB 1546 mirrors it. Colorado's AI
Act took effect June 2026 for consequential decisions. Utah requires disclosure at the start of
a high-risk consumer interaction.

**Freshness.** Three days old at time of writing, and it is a statute rather than a survey, so
it cannot be a re-circulated stat. This is the best provenance available to this file: primary
law with a commencement date, not a vendor's blog post about someone else's research.

> **Honesty flags, all three must be handled in the script.**
> 1. The confirmed ICP is US and UK. The AI Act binds anyone whose AI interacts with people
>    **in the EU**, so a US or UK founder selling into Europe is in scope and one selling
>    purely domestically is not. Do not tell a Texas founder with Texas customers that Brussels
>    is coming for them. The US hook is SB 243 and it is strong enough on its own.
> 2. The federal **Stealth Bot Prohibition Act was introduced in July 2026 and is not law.**
>    It is still in committee. Describing it as passed is precisely the "stale claim dressed as
>    news" failure this file exists to prevent.
> 3. Do not imply Ultron makes you compliant. It gives the agent an identity and holds the
>    send; compliance is the operator's, and COUNSEL drafts, it does not certify.

**ICP survival.** Very good, and unusually concrete. This is not an enterprise statistic that
has to be translated down. A two-person company running AI outbound is a deployer with the same
obligation as a two-thousand person one, and the private right of action in California means the
counterparty does not need a regulator to act.

**Ultron mapping.** Direct and structural, and it is the one unspent product surface in the
file: **BCP, the Business Common Profile - the identity/directory layer for the agentic web**
(app.51ultron.com/bcp). Article 50 asks an agent to identify itself and say who it represents.
That is what a BCP entry is. The mechanism is a feature that exists and has a confirmed URL,
which is check 4 satisfied without rhetoric.

**Proposed angle.** Not "here is a compliance checklist", which nobody saves. The angle is
identity as an asset: *the agents that get answered are the ones that say who sent them, and
that stopped being a courtesy on 2 August.* The law is the reason it is urgent; being answered
is the reason anyone cares.
**Keyword.** SENDER.
**Collision.** 04 spent governance and HUMAN GATE as the punchline, and this file blocked
candidate 5 until "no sooner than two episodes out, and then only from the COUNSEL angle
(contract and liability), not the approval-gate angle." Episodes 05, 06 and 07 have shipped
since, so the cooldown is served, and this is the liability angle rather than the approval one.
The payoff is **identity (BCP)**, not approval, which keeps it clean against 04.
