## What "the run-board model" is, structurally

Episodes 05, 10, 11 and 12 share one shape:

| Element | Episode 05 |
|---|---|
| Hook | `*Claude* turned\|a cold list\|into a *paying*\|customer.` - Claude named, one concrete transformation, four lines, no statistic |
| Beat 1 | Hook sentence, then the proof clause: "It took 48 hours and I never wrote an email" |
| Beats 2-5 | **Numbered spans of elapsed time**, each carrying exactly ONE concrete action |
| Beat 6 | "Comment 48H and I will send you the exact run" |
| Objects | Clay, one per beat: dial, board, pay, phone |
| Voice | Operator debrief, past tense, no hedging, and the actor is always **"it"** |

The spine is a **run**: a named window of time, split into numbered phases, one thing per
phase, ending on the outcome.

## Subject

**The seven days after a customer pays.** The deal is closed, the invoice is sent, and the
founder moves on to the next one. This is the exact moment churn starts, because nobody is
watching whether the customer actually got what they paid for.

- **Keyword** WEEK. Free, checked against every shipped film keyword.
- **Collision** Clean. No shipped film covers post-sale onboarding or retention.
- **ICP** Direct. A founder at 2 to 50 people carries the whole relationship and has no
  customer success role to hand it to.

The story is not "the software prevents churn." It is "the founder forgets the customer the
moment they pay, and the software is the one that does not."

---

## Numbers

Same universe as the shipped films. One week, one new customer, one signal the founder would
not have caught.

Nothing here claims a retention rate, a churn benchmark, or revenue saved. The outcome the
film claims is the one the product performs: the onboarding checklist gets built from the
deal notes, engagement gets watched, and the founder knows which account needs attention
before it becomes a cancellation email.

## Script

**Hook** (four lines, the run shape)

    *Claude* tracked|the customer|I had already|*forgotten*.

**Beat 1** - Claude tracked the customer I had already forgotten. It took seven days and
I did not open the dashboard once.

**Beat 2** - Day one. It turns the deal notes into an onboarding checklist, so nothing I
promised gets dropped.

**Beat 3** - Days two and three. It watches engagement against that checklist, and flags
the one account that is already behind.

**Beat 4** - Days four and five. It drafts the check-in from the signal, in my own words,
so I sound like I noticed.

**Beat 5** - Days six and seven. The week-end summary arrives with who is healthy, who is
not, and what I send next.

**Beat 6** - Comment WEEK and I will send you the exact run.

**Accent words:** seven, forgotten, checklist, behind, signal, noticed, healthy, send

**Product line (caption, not VO):** This is the Ultron platform, one input and a team that
runs behind it.

## What this film may not say

- No agent names. The actor is "it", the platform is Ultron, the model is Claude.
- No retention rate, no churn percentage, no revenue saved, no benchmark.
- No suggestion that anything sends itself. The founder sends the check-in. That is beat 4's
  whole point.
- No claim that the software handles onboarding *for* the customer. It builds the checklist
  from deal notes and watches engagement. The founder executes.
