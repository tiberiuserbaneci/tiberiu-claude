Your profile is the foundation agents build on. Before Specter can find the right leads, it needs to know your ICP. Before Pulse can write in your voice, it needs your tone and samples. Before Striker can handle objections, it needs your pricing and case studies.

Getting your profile right is the highest-leverage thing you can do.

## Business Profile

Go to **Dashboard → Settings → Business Profile**.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Business description">
    What do you sell and who do you sell it to? Write 2-4 sentences.

    **Bad:** "We help businesses grow."

    **Good:** "We build AI-powered workflow tools for operations teams at B2B SaaS companies with 50-500 employees. Our software replaces manual data entry and coordination tasks, typically saving ops teams 10-15 hours per week."

    This text is injected into every agent's context. The more specific, the more accurately agents can position you in research, outreach, and content.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Ideal Customer Profile (ICP)">
    Your ICP is the single most important input for Specter. Be specific.

    **Bad:** "Small business owners"

    **Good:** "B2B SaaS product managers at companies with 50-500 employees, Series A or B funded, using HubSpot or Salesforce, headquartered in the US or UK. Decision-maker or strong influencer on tool purchases."

    Specter uses this to build search queries, score leads 0-100, and write personalized outreach angles. A vague ICP produces generic leads. A specific ICP produces the right leads.

    **ICP fields to include:**
    - Job title(s)
    - Company size (employee range)
    - Industry/vertical
    - Funding stage or revenue range
    - Geographic focus
    - Tech stack signals (if relevant)
    - What they're typically trying to solve
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Competitors">
    List your main competitors by name (and URL if helpful).

    Cortex uses this to run competitive audits, build battle cards, and find their customers as your prospects. Striker uses it when handling objections in sales conversations.

    **Example:** "Main competitors: HubSpot Sales Hub, Apollo.io, Salesloft. We often lose deals to Apollo on price and to Salesloft on enterprise features."
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Pricing">
    What do you charge? Include tier names, price points, and what each includes.

    Striker uses this when writing proposals. Pulse uses it when writing promotional content. Cortex uses it for competitive positioning analysis.

    **Example:** "Starter: $49/month (up to 3 users, core features). Growth: $149/month (up to 10 users, full suite). Enterprise: custom pricing, typically $500-2,000/month."
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Case studies and results">
    What specific results have clients achieved? Include numbers where you have them.

    **Example:** "Acme Corp: reduced manual reporting time by 12 hours/week after 6 weeks. TechStart: onboarded 3 new clients in first month with zero additional headcount. DataFirm: cut their sales cycle from 45 days to 22 days."

    Striker uses these in proposals and outreach. Pulse uses them in case study content. More specifics = more credible output.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Common objections and rebuttals">
    What pushback do you hear most often? What's your response?

    **Example:**
    - "It's too expensive" → "Most clients see positive ROI within 6 weeks. Here's how Acme calculated theirs..."
    - "We already have HubSpot" → "We actually integrate with HubSpot. Most clients use us alongside it for the automation layer HubSpot doesn't cover."

    Striker uses these directly when triaging inbox conversations and building battle cards.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Voice & Tone Settings

Go to **Dashboard → Settings → Voice & Tone**.

How you write matters. Pulse needs to sound like you — not like generic AI. Voice calibration is what makes that possible.

### Tone descriptor

Write 5-10 words that describe your writing style:

- "Direct, no-BS, practical examples over theory"
- "Thoughtful, data-driven, slightly contrarian, long sentences"
- "Casual and conversational, founder-to-founder, use humor occasionally"

### Voice samples

Paste 2-3 of your best-performing posts. Choose pieces that:
- Performed well (lots of comments or saves)
- Sound most like how you actually talk
- Represent the tone you want to replicate

Pulse analyzes: sentence length distribution, vocabulary level, hook structure, CTA style, use of questions, formatting patterns.

The more samples you provide, the better the calibration.

### Platforms

Select which platforms you publish on: LinkedIn, Twitter/X, Instagram, Substack, Blog, Email newsletter.

Pulse adapts format, length, and structure for each platform. A LinkedIn post and a Twitter thread require completely different approaches.

## Account Settings

Go to **Dashboard → Settings → Account**.

- Change your name and email
- Update your password
- View your workspace code (e.g., `UTR-A3F9K2`) — used to connect mobile channels

## When profile changes take effect

Changes to your business profile take effect on your next chat conversation. Changes to voice settings take effect on Pulse's next content task. There's no delay — just save and your next interaction will use the updated context.

<Tip>
  Start with ICP and voice samples. These two fields have the biggest impact on output quality. You can fill in the rest over time as you see what agents produce.
</Tip>
