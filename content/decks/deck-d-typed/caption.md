## LINKEDIN CAPTION  (carousel 1080x1350)

Seven things typed into Claude in plain English. No tricks, no prompt engineering, no roles
assigned. The seventh one took my pipeline from 41 open deals to 9 real ones in a minute.

- - -

Every post about prompts is about prompts. None of them are about the seven sentences a
founder actually types on a Tuesday, which are boring, short, and written the way you would
say them to a person. That is the point. If an input needs a technique to work, it is going to
stop working the week the model changes. These seven have survived every model change since I
started, because they are just clear questions about my own business.

- - -

→ Who has this problem this quarter, and who signs? Comes back as 40 companies ranked by why
now rather than alphabetically, with the signer named and sources attached to every line.

→ Write the first line from something they actually posted. One real trigger per company,
quoted, under 60 words, and the ask is a question. Forty different openings, not one template.

→ Read this and tell me what is not standard. On a 14 page contract that comes back as 3
clauses that are not market, why each one matters to me, and the redline in my own words.

→ Does this job need the expensive model? A lookup with one right answer goes to Haiku 4.5 at
$1 and $5. Real judgement goes to Opus 5 at $5 and $25. Deciding per turn is most of the bill.

→ Of my 41 open deals, which would you still work on Monday? 22 had not moved in a month and
nobody had said no. 10 were a person who likes me and cannot sign. 9 were real.

- - -

What all seven have in common is that none of them ask for a decision. They ask for the
reading, the ranking and the first draft, and then they stop. It ranks, I decide. It drafts, I
send. Nothing has ever left unread, and that gate is not a limitation I put up reluctantly. It
is the reason my name still works. The other thing they have in common is that every one of
them is a question I would have asked a person if I had one. That is the actual test I use
now, and it has replaced every prompt guide I ever bookmarked: if you would not say the
sentence out loud to a competent colleague, it is not a good input, it is a costume. The
seven above take about nine seconds each to type and none of them have needed rewriting in
six months.
