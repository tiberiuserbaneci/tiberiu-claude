## LINKEDIN CAPTION  (carousel 1080x1350)

A rule that depends on you remembering it at 6pm on a Thursday is not a rule. It is an
intention, and Claude is the only reason four of mine survived a whole year.

- - -

These are not advice and I am not sure all seven are right. They are what I actually run on,
written down after a year of running on nothing, and I have already dropped two that sounded
excellent and did not survive contact with a real week. Post them and somebody always tells me
which one is wrong for their business, which is the useful part. So the same offer stands here:
if one of these is wrong, say which one and why, and I will argue back or change my mind.

- - -

→ Monday starts with the one thing only I could have done. If I cannot name it before I open
anything, the day already belongs to somebody else and it is 11am before I notice.

→ Nothing goes out unread. Everything is written, ranked and queued, and the gate costs me
about 10 minutes. That 10 minutes is the entire reason my name still works.

→ Quiet is not alive. Nobody says no any more, so a deal with no reply in a month is not
pending. It is finished, and it is holding a slot that a real one could have.

→ If I am editing heavily, the brief was wrong, not the output. Fix the thing one step back
rather than the same output every single time. This is the rule I break most.

→ A lookup has one right answer and judgement does not. Matching the job to the tier per job,
rather than once months ago, is most of the difference on the bill.

- - -

The seventh one took longest to learn. A good result that lives in a chat window nobody
reopens did not happen. If something worked, it has to become a thing that runs again next
week without me awake for it, or it was a nice afternoon and nothing else. Most of what
founders call leverage is a good afternoon they never repeated. Split them and the pattern is
uncomfortable: the four that hold are the four where something other than my memory keeps
score, and the three that are purely mine are the three I break, usually on the day I most
needed them. That is not a discipline problem. It is what happens to anything stored only in
a tired person.
