- - -

Most of what gets written about AI and founders is a list of things it can do, which is not
useful, because the question is never what it can do. The question is which of the jobs in your
week are still yours. I sat down and split mine honestly, seven jobs, and the split is not the
one I would have guessed a year ago. Two handed over completely. Two are shared, which means it
drafts and I decide. Three are still mine and I do not expect that to change, because they are
the ones where being wrong costs something no output quality can recover.

- - -

→ Finding who to write to. Not mine any more. 40 companies with a reason to care this quarter
and the person who signs at each was an afternoon a week, and it is the cleanest handover on
the list.

→ Ranking the pipeline. Not mine. What moved, who can sign, what is stale. 4 hours a week to
produce a number, and now the number is there before I sit down on a Sunday.

→ The first line. Shared. It drafts 40, each from a real trigger, and I read them and press
send. That gate costs 10 minutes and it is why my name still works.

→ The contract. Shared. Three clauses that are not market and why each matters to me. I sign
it, it does not, and none of it is legal advice.

→ Deciding what a reply means. Still mine. It can tell me what they are protecting. It cannot
tell me whether I want this customer.

- - -

The last two are the ones I would defend hardest. Saying no to a customer, because the wording
is easy and choosing which relationship to keep is not. And deciding what we build next, where
it ranks the complaints by how many people made them, which is genuinely useful, and then stops
at exactly the point that matters: choosing between two good options on incomplete information.
That is the job. Everything above it was overhead I mistook for the job for about a year,
and the mistake lasted a year because overhead feels like work in a way the real decisions
never do. Ranking a pipeline for four hours produces a visible artifact and a sense of
having been thorough. Choosing between two roadmap options produces nothing you can show
anybody and takes twenty minutes of genuine discomfort. Guess which one I reached for on a
Friday afternoon, every week, for most of a year.
