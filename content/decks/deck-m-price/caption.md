Nobody objected to my price for eleven months and I read that as a compliment. Claude was the

Pricing is the only decision a founder makes repeatedly with no feedback loop, which is why
mine was wrong three times in three different directions and I did not notice any of them at
the time. The first was too low because I priced from my costs. The second was too flat
because I had one number for two buyers who were nothing alike. The third was not the number
at all, it was that I explained it. Every one of those decisions felt careful when I made it,
and every one of them was fear with a spreadsheet attached.

- - -

→ Priced from my costs, which the buyer has never seen and does not care about. What they were
already paying to get this done badly was 4x what I was asking.

→ 0 objections in 11 months. Zero pushback means you are not in the conversation they are
having. The test is to raise it on the next 3 and watch, which costs nothing you were getting
anyway.

→ Discounted 30 percent on the first three deals. The first price you accept becomes the
price, because they talk to each other and so do their finance teams.

→ 1 price for two buyers doing two different jobs, so one of them was always wrong. Claude
ranked which deals belonged to which job and the split had been sitting in the pipeline for a
year.

→ 9 paragraphs of justification on a pricing page. Every line of it told them to expect an
argument, so they went and looked for the argument.

- - -

The two I still find hardest are the honest ones. Moving the price quietly, twice, and being
found out both times, when telling people first with a reason and a date cost nothing and lost
nobody. And selling on price at all, which brought exactly one customer who chose me for being
cheapest and lost exactly one for the same reason, inside a quarter. Whoever arrives for the
number leaves for the number. That is not cynicism, it is just what the number selects for,
and it selects for it every time, without any regard for how good the product turns out to
be. The reason all three of my prices were wrong in different directions is that I kept
solving for how the number felt to say out loud rather than for what it was competing with.
