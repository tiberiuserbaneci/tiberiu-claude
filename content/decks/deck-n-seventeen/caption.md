Three of my seventeen rejections were not rejections at all. Claude found that the moment all
seventeen sat in one place, and I had been carrying them around separately for a year.

- - -

A no arrives on its own, at the end of a long day, and it feels like a verdict on the whole
company. Seventeen of them arrived that way before anyone said yes. What I never did until
this year was put them in one document and read them together, which turns out to be a
completely different activity from receiving them. Sorted, most of them stop being rejections
and start being information, and two of the seven categories are not about you at all.

- - -

→ 4 said not now, with no budget cycle named and no date attached. That is not a not now, it
is a no with better manners, and 3 of the 4 never came back.

→ 3 had built it internally, badly, and were never going to admit that to a stranger. This is
the one that becomes a yes about eighteen months later, when the person who built it leaves.

→ 3 were the wrong person. Interested, encouraging, unable to sign anything. Those were not
rejections, they were me writing to whoever answered.

→ 2 said too expensive, which surprised me because price is the objection founders fear most.
Both meant it was not worth it to them, which is a value problem in a price costume.

→ 2 had real reasons, a funding round and a round of redundancies, and both named a date to
come back. One of them is a customer now, 14 months later.

- - -

The last two are the honest ones. Two people went silent after a good call, which is the kind
that keeps you awake, and the truthful answer is that something changed on their side that had
nothing to do with me. And one company should never have been on my list at all, which was my
error rather than their no. Out of seventeen, exactly two were somebody deciding against the
thing itself. I would have guessed twelve, and that gap between what I felt and what was
there is why this is worth half an evening. Nothing about the seventeen changed. What
changed is that they stopped being a mood I carried into every call and became seven
categories, five of which have an obvious next action attached. A no you cannot act on is
a weight. A no in a column is a task, and there is no version of a founder's week where
those two things cost the same.
