and I would give the same number again. The mistake is that a feature is not a task, it is a
liability you agree to keep, and none of the lines that make it expensive are visible in the
moment somebody asks you nicely on a call. So I itemised one. Every hour, including the ones
that arrive after it ships and never stop arriving.

- - -

→ 2 days building, which is the only line I estimated and the only one I got right.

→ 1 day of rework, because the request was one sentence in a call and nobody wrote down what
done meant. Heavy editing always means the brief was wrong, not the output.

→ 1 day testing the paths that are not the happy one. Skipped, this line does not disappear.
It moves to a customer finding it on a Friday afternoon.

→ 3 days spent by the next feature working around this one. That is what a feature actually
is: not a thing you add, a constraint you agree to keep forever.

→ 1 day a month, supporting it, for as long as the company exists. This is the line that
changes the arithmetic, and it is completely invisible on the day you say yes.
