Forty follow ups over four months and not one of them got a reply, which I read as a market
problem for an embarrassingly long time. It was not. Every single one of those forty was a
message about me, sent on a schedule, giving the person nothing they could answer. The deals
were already there. I had done the hard part, which is getting somebody to take a call, and
then I spent four months undoing it politely.

- - -

→ 0 replies from 40 messages that said just checking in. It gives them nothing to answer, so
answering is pure cost with no reason attached.

→ Following up 3 days after a call, before anything could possibly have changed. Nothing has
changed in three days except that you now look anxious.

→ A 5 email sequence where all 5 were about me, arriving on a schedule that is obviously a
schedule. Two specific ones beat five generic ones every time.

→ 6 weeks following up with somebody who could not sign anything. A champion who cannot sign is
a friend, and six weeks of friendship is not a pipeline.

→ Giving up after 2 attempts, when the yes usually arrives on the fifth. Two is exactly where
the pile of unworked deals begins.

- - -

The fix is not persistence and it is not a tool that sends more. Five follow ups only work if
each one carries something the last one did not: a new customer like them, a thing they asked
about that shipped, a number that changed. The moment I cannot think of a new reason to write,
that is the real signal to stop, and it is a much better signal than a counter. Most founders
send five reminders and call it five follow ups. They are not the same thing and the recipient
can tell instantly.
The part worth saying to anybody still stopping at two: the deals are not gone. Mine were
sitting there the whole time, and the ones I reopened with a genuinely new reason replied at
a rate that made the previous four months look like a choice rather than a market. Getting
somebody to take a call is the hard part and you have already done it. What happens
afterwards is admin you can either do badly or hand over, and for a year I did it badly and
called it respecting their time.
