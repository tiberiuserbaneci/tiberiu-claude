## LINKEDIN CAPTION  (carousel 1080x1350)

Most of my first hour is reading what Claude already did while I was asleep, which is why the
whole thing takes one hour forty rather than a morning.

- - -

Every morning routine post is about cold water and journalling, and none of them are about the
only question that matters, which is whether you arrive at 9am having decided the day or having
inherited it. Mine is not a routine and there is nothing aspirational in it. It is triage:
seven blocks, six twenty to eight, and the whole design goal is that nothing creative and
nothing optional happens inside it.

- - -

→ 06:20, the phone stays shut for 20 minutes. Not discipline, arithmetic. Whatever is in there
was already there at 4am and none of it improves by being read half awake.

→ 06:40, one page of what ran overnight. What went out, what came back, what needs me. It is
assembled while I am asleep, which is why this block is 4 minutes rather than 40.

→ 06:45, name the one thing only I can do, written down before anything else opens. If I cannot
name it, the day belongs to somebody else and I find that out at 11am.

→ 07:00, replies in one pass, in order of who can sign. Not the inbox. The 7 that matter,
drafted and waiting on me to read them.

→ 07:25, the hard conversation, first, while I still have the appetite for it. Moved to the
afternoon it becomes tomorrow, and tomorrow it becomes next week.

- - -

The block nobody expects is 07:45: nothing new before eight. No new ideas, no new tools, no new
tabs. Anything arriving in those fifteen minutes gets written down and read after lunch, because
a new idea at 7:45 is not urgency, it is avoidance wearing a good suit, and it is aimed
precisely at the hard thing I scheduled twenty minutes earlier. By eight the day is decided.
Nothing in that hour was optional and nothing in it was clever, and by nine I am doing the work
rather than choosing it.
The obvious objection is that it sounds rigid, and it is the opposite. A fixed hour at the
front is what makes the other ten flexible, because nothing important is left waiting to be
decided in them. The founders I know with genuinely chaotic days are not the disciplined ones
and the loose ones. They are the ones whose first decision of the day happens at 11am, by
which point the day has already been allocated by whoever emailed first.
