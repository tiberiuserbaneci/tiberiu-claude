## LINKEDIN CAPTION  (carousel 1080x1350)

- - -

Launch day was 41 visitors and 7 of them were me. Four months of building and one afternoon of
telling people, which is the ratio almost every first launch has and almost nobody posts. What
I got wrong was not the product and not the timing. It was believing a launch is an event. A
launch is a day, and distribution is a habit, and the week I am about to describe is the week I
found out which of the two I had.

- - -

→ Day 2, nobody signed up, so I opened the code. Rebuilding is what founders do when they are
frightened, because it is the part they are good at. I closed it again.

→ Day 3, I wrote 12 individual messages by hand, no template. 9 replied and not one of them had
seen the post. Reach is not distribution.

→ Day 4, somebody said no and told me why, which is worth more than the yes I was hoping for.
It was the pricing page. It explained what the product does and never once what it replaces.

→ Day 5, Claude read every reply from those 12 and wrote the page from their words. Nothing
about the product changed.

→ Day 6, one signup from somebody I had never met. That is the whole difference between a demo
and a business, and it landed on the version written from somebody else's objection.

- - -

By day 7 I had stopped calling it a launch, which sounds like a small thing and was not. A
launch gives you one day to be found and then punishes you for the silence afterwards. A habit
gives you a morning, every morning, where the first thing that happens is writing to people. My
week only worked because day 3 existed, and day 3 was not on the plan. It was panic, and panic
happened to point in the right direction for once. The version of this I would run now puts
those twelve names on paper before the post goes up, because the launch is not what finds
people. It only tells the people you already found that the thing is finally real, and if that
list is empty on the morning of day one then the silence on day two is arithmetic rather than
rejection.
