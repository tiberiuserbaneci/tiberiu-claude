# deck-a-year  ·  keyword YEAR

Four pieces. The three hooks are deliberately different: the VISUAL says the count, the
REACTION asks what the year bought, the CAPTION says the tool was never the problem, the
FIRST COMMENT names the single worst number. Nobody reads the same sentence twice.

## LINKEDIN CAPTION  (carousel 1080x1350)

Nine months of building, and Claude could not have saved a single one of them.
The tool was never the thing that cost me the year.

- - -

Year one ended at eleven at night, every night, and I could not have told you what I had
actually built. So I went back through twelve months of calendar, inbox and deal notes and
counted. Seven decisions, every one of them reasonable on the day I made it, every one of them
expensive in a way I only saw from the other side. Not one was a decision about which tool to
use. All seven were decisions about where my attention went, which is the only budget a
founder actually controls in year one. The uncomfortable part is that I would have defended
every single one of them at the time, and four of them I was proud of.

- - -

→ 41 open deals, and 9 of them were real. A reply feels like a deal. Quiet feels like alive.
Neither is true, and I worked all 41 as if they were.

→ 9 months building before I sold anything, for a customer who did not exist yet. Every
feature felt like progress. None of it was evidence.

→ 60 hours a week, and the hours were the only thing that scaled. Eleven at night is not
commitment. It is the absence of leverage, and I wore it like a credential.

→ 10 minutes at the start of every single session re-explaining my own company, because I
never wrote the context down once. Over a year that is not ten minutes. It is weeks.

→ 1 bad line, sent to a hundred people, with my name on it. Volume is easy. Getting your name
back afterwards is not, and that was the one that nearly ended it.

- - -

None of the seven were fixed by working harder, and none of them were fixed by buying a tool
either. Every one of them was the same mistake wearing a different coat: I kept doing the work
that came before the judgement, and then I had no judgement left for the part that needed it.
They were fixed by deciding what only I could do and letting everything upstream of that run
without me in the room. Claude does the reading, the ranking and the first draft now. I read it
once and I press send. That gate is the whole difference between leverage and a hundred people
receiving something I would never have written with my name on it.
