- - -

There is no framework here and no course. Month eleven, 02:14, and everyone I could have asked
either works for me, invested in me, or is married to me. None of those three people give you
an unflattering answer at that hour, and they should not have to. So I typed the questions
instead, and the thing I did not expect was how much of the value was in the pushback rather
than the reply. It has nothing invested in how I feel when I close the laptop, which turns out
to be the whole qualification for the job.

- - -

→ Am I too late? Four funded competitors felt like a verdict. Funded means they must grow
fast, not that they serve anyone well, and late matters in a race rather than a fit question.

→ Of my open deals, which would you still work on Monday? 22 had not moved in a month with
nobody saying no. 10 were a person who likes me and cannot sign. 9 were real.

→ Argue against my own plan. What came back was the 3 assumptions the whole thing rests on,
which one is cheapest to test this week, and what evidence would actually change my mind.

→ Nobody has pushed back on my price. What does that tell you? That zero pushback is a signal
and it is not a good one, and the test is to raise it on the next three and watch.

→ Here is my calendar, what did I actually do this week? Hours by what they moved rather than
what they were called, and the recurring meeting that has produced nothing in 6 weeks.

- - -

The last one was give me the case for shutting this down. It made the case, properly, without
softening it, and then asked what would have to be true in ninety days for it to work and
which of those I could actually influence. I am still here, and I am still here because of the
answer rather than in spite of it. There is a difference between being talked out of quitting
and being shown the version of the argument you were too tired to construct.
