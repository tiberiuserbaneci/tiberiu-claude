Sunday evening, twenty minutes, seven numbers. That is the whole review, and it replaced a
weekly planning ritual I never once acted on. The difference is not the format. It is that
every one of these seven names a decision rather than describing a state, so if a number is
wrong I know exactly what Monday is for before Monday starts. A dashboard that tells you how
things are going is entertainment. A number that tells you what to do this week is a review.

- - -

→ Deals that moved this week. Not how many are open, how many did something a person can point
to. Zero moved means the week was admin, not selling, whatever it felt like at the time.

→ Deals with a named signer. A contact is not a buyer. If nobody in the thread can sign, the
deal has not started yet, whatever the notes say about it.

→ Days since the oldest open deal moved. The number nobody wants to look at. Past 30 days it is
not pending, it is finished, and it is holding a slot.

→ First replies this week, not sends. Sends measure effort and effort has never been the
constraint. If sends are up and replies are flat, the message is wrong, not the volume.

→ Hours on work only I could do. Judgement and relationships. Under 5 hours in a week means I
spent it being staff at my own company, and that has happened more often than I would like.

- - -

Number six is what the AI spend actually bought, which is cost per thing that shipped rather
than the total. If the cost is climbing and the output is flat, that is a routing problem, not
a usage problem. And number seven is the only one that is a sentence: one thing I will not do
next week. If nothing comes off the list, next week is this week with more on it, and that is
how a year disappears without a single bad decision in it. The reason the other six are worth

attention left is the difference between planning a week and inheriting one. I spent year one
doing this review by hand on the Sundays I had the energy for it, which is to say about nine
Sundays out of fifty two, and the reviews I skipped are exactly the weeks I cannot account for
