## CAPTION

Your AI tool runs a quick lookup on the same expensive model it uses to write a contract. You pay contract prices for a search, every single time.

That one default is why most AI feels slow and somehow always over budget.

I did not want one model for everything. A founder runs a dozen different jobs in a day, and they are not the same difficulty. Finding a lead is not reviewing an NDA. So every job goes through a router first, and the router picks the model that fits the work, not the most expensive one on the shelf.

Here is how the three tiers split the day:

→ Lite runs the lookups. Build a live lead list, qualify a lead, enrich a contact, sort replies, schedule a post. Fast, cheap, good enough, and it never burns a deep model on a search.

→ Smart runs the daily work. Write 50 cold emails, post in your voice, draft a follow-up sequence, summarize a call. This is the default for real output, the workhorse tier.

→ Deep runs the calls that matter. Research an account, handle an objection and the proposal, build or fix a page, review an NDA, plan a launch. Slower and pricier, used only where judgement actually pays for itself.

→ You pay per token, so most tasks land in cents. The expensive tier is reserved for the few jobs that earn it, not spent by accident on everything.

The lesson took me a while. The win is not a smarter model, it is sending each job to the right one. Same chat, same context, three brains behind it, and the bill stops being a surprise.

A tool that runs everything on one model is easy to build and expensive to run. A router that matches the model to the work is the difference between a demo and something you run a company on.
