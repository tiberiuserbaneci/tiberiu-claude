## CAPTION

Most founders buy lead lists that were exported a year ago. The good ones point an AI at the map and pull the list live, phone and website already filled in.

- - -

I run outbound for a small company, and the bottleneck was never the email. It was the list. Every bought list came back with dead numbers and missing websites, so half the work was cleaning it before I could send a single message. The last list I paid for had a dead-number rate near 40 percent, and I only found out after the first batch bounced. So I wired an AI scraper that pulls local businesses straight off the map, on demand, the moment I need them.

- - -

→ One input. A category and a city. dentist in Austin, max 500. That is the whole brief.
→ One row per place. Name, address, phone, website, categories, rating, review count, hours, photo count. Nine fields, every business.
→ Two modes. Fast for names and ratings when you just want the map, full for the phone, website and stable place id when you are going to reach out.
→ Up to 500 per search, on a residential proxy that is on by default, so Google does not treat it like a bot and block it.
→ It waits for each field to load before it reads, so no row comes back half-empty, and you get one clean file at the end.
→ It costs cents per thousand rows, so a clean local list stops being a budget line and becomes a checkbox before every campaign.

This is the map as it is today, with the number you actually dial and the site you actually open. And the leads do not sit in a spreadsheet. They flow straight into the outbound to be enriched and contacted, end to end, with no copy and paste in the middle. You run it the morning you need leads, against the map as it stands that day, not against a file someone exported six months ago and forgot existed. That is the whole difference between a list and a lead.
