## CAPTION

Most founders think a GTM engine needs five hires.
Mine runs on five AI agents and one approval step.

- - -

For a year I told myself the next hire would fix my pipeline. It never did. The pipeline stayed thin while I stayed busy, and a founder running GTM alone does not get a second pair of hands by wishing for one. So last month I stopped waiting for headcount and gave myself a single weekend. The goal was blunt: stand up the entire go-to-market motion, from a cold list to a live outbound sequence, using Claude and the Ultron agents, and see how far one operator could get by Sunday night. I assumed I would finish the research and maybe a handful of emails. By Sunday the whole loop was running and the only job left for me was to approve it.

- - -

Here is the exact 5-step build, one agent per job.

→ CORTEX mapped the market into one ranked brief. Founder-led companies, 2 to 50 people, US and UK, sorted by fit and trigger from 96 down to 81. I started Saturday with the right 50, not a dump of 5,000.

→ SPECTER turned that brief into a 4-touch sequence over 12 days. Cold email, follow-up, value drop, break-up, every one drafted before I finished my coffee.

→ STRIKER ran the deal end to end. Qualify, discovery, objection, proposal, close plan. It had the answer to "too expensive" written before the call, tied to the pipeline it replaces instead of the price.

→ PULSE wrote one post in my voice and AMPLIFY reshaped it for LinkedIn, TikTok and X, then scheduled each one for its own time zone.

→ The ROUTER tied it together. I typed plain English, it picked the agent and the model tier, and the HUMAN GATE held every send until I approved it.

- - -

The unlock was never a smarter model. A better model on its own changed almost nothing for me last year. The shift was refusing to be the bottleneck on work that does not need a human in the loop. Five agents did the reps. I kept the one job that should never be automated, deciding what is allowed to leave the building. Notice what that split actually means. The work that scales is the work a human never has to touch, and the work that matters is the one call a human always makes. That is the whole gap between a tool you babysit and an operator you approve.
