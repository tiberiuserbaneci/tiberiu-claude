I stopped treating my AI like one overworked generalist. I split the work the way a real company does, into seven heads of department, each with one job and the skills to do it. Now I do not prompt a chatbot. I type a slash and call the department I need.

Here is the roster:

→ /cortex runs intelligence: research, competitor teardowns, pricing.
→ /specter runs acquisition: finds the leads and writes the outreach.
→ /striker runs growth: pre-call prep, objection handling, follow-through.
→ /pulse runs marketing: posts and blogs in your own voice.
→ /sentinel runs engineering: builds, ships and tests internal tools.
→ /amplify runs paid media: campaigns, audiences, creative, bidding.
→ /counsel runs legal: drafts and reviews your contracts.

Seven units, 56 skills underneath, one slash command to reach each one.

The trick was never a smarter model. It was clear ownership. When every job has one head with the right skills, the work stops being generic and starts being yours. The router picks the model tier for each task, so you are not paying deep-reasoning prices for a quick lookup. And nothing ships until you approve it.

You are still the operator. You just stopped doing all seven jobs yourself.
