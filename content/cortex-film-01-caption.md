## LINKEDIN CAPTION

(CLAUDE.md 15.1: 5 blocks, 400-470 words, hook differs from the visual hook)

Most founder research is four browser tabs and a blank page.
Claude closed all four before I finished reading the first one.

- - -

Research is the tax you pay before any deal starts. LinkedIn for the person, the company site for the pitch, Crunchbase for the money, news for the timing. Four tabs, the better part of an hour, and you still have not written a word. So I rebuilt that entire step as one input field and handed it to CORTEX, the research agent in the Ultron stack.

- - -

→ One domain goes in. No list to build, no filters to set, no enrichment tool to configure. The input is the only thing you already know: where they live on the internet.

→ CORTEX reads the company in five passes. Sector, size band, region, buying signals, and the stack they already pay for. My confirmed ICP is founders at 2 to 50 employee companies in IT services, software and consulting, across the US and UK, so those are the fields it scores against instead of a generic firmographic dump.

→ Then it reads the one person who signs. Not the whole org chart. The human who owns the GTM budget, what they posted this month, and the angle that fits what they actually said.

→ It ranks the output into one page with three sections. Why they buy now. Who actually signs. The line that opens the email, written from their words instead of yours.

→ The model tier is picked per job, not per user. Lite for the lookups, Deep for the judgement call on fit. You never choose a model, and you never pay for the heavy one on a task that did not need it.

- - -

The lesson underneath is that research was never the hard part. Reading was. Four tabs give you four disconnected facts and no ranking, so you write a generic opener and then blame the copy when nobody replies. One ranked brief gives you a reason to send the email at all. The agent is not faster at browsing than you are. It is faster at deciding what matters.
