## CAPTION

32 turns of Claude research, 3 agents and one comparison table. I handed all of it to a prospect with a single read-only link, not a screenshot.

For two years I shared my AI work the worst way possible. A screenshot pasted into an email.

- - -

The other side saw a frozen image. No sources, no context, no way to pick it up and keep going. Then I stopped screenshotting and started sending the session itself. One link, read-only, the whole thing. A prospect sees the research I ran on their market. A teammate gets the draft and the agents behind it. My audience watches the actual work, not a tidy summary of it.

- - -

→ The link carries the whole session. Every turn in order, which agent ran on which tier, the canvas tables and drafts as live artifacts, and every cited source still clicking through. Not an image, the real thing.

→ It is read-only by default. They read every turn and open the artifacts. They cannot send a message in my session, edit it, or see my account or other chats. The link is scoped to that one session.

→ I keep the controls. Revoke in one click and the link dies instantly. Set an expiry, add a password for sensitive sessions, and the scope never widens past that chat.

→ Fork branches a session into two without losing the thread. I keep the proven thread untouched and explore a riskier angle in a copy, then switch between them.

→ Anyone can clone a shared session into their own Ultron and keep going, if I leave cloning on. The link they opened to view quietly becomes their first working session, with my whole thread as the starting point.

- - -

A screenshot is where your AI work goes to die. A shared session is an asset. It travels, it proves the work, and every link you send is a front door someone can walk through and build from. For a founder selling trust, that is everything: the prospect does not take your word for the research, they open it and check every source themselves. That is the difference between showing people a result and handing them the machine that made it.
