## CAPTION

The product took months. The sentence standing in front of it took an afternoon between two
calls, and Claude now writes that sentence better than I do.

That order is backwards and almost everybody runs it. I spent the better part of a year on a
product I am genuinely proud of, then wrote the cold email announcing it in the gap between two
meetings, sent it, and never read it again. The email is the only part most people ever meet.
I went back and read every piece of writing standing in front of the product, and none of the
problems were engineering.

- - -

Three things were broken, and all three were writing:

→ The first line said what we do, not why now. "We help companies like yours" says nothing
about them, and it is the line the reader decides on.

→ The page they open explained the product, not the problem. Someone arriving cold does not
know which of their own problems this is for, and a feature list will not tell them.

→ The follow up was never sent. Not written badly. Not sent at all, because writing it a second
time felt like admitting the first one failed.

- - -

The fix was not a copywriting course. It was one paragraph, plain English: who it is for, and
what breaks for them without it. Two sentences, no adjectives, and it took me four attempts
because writing plainly about your own product is harder than describing it. That paragraph is
the only asset. Claude reads the product, the posts and the past deals, then writes the first
line, the page and the follow up out of it, in my voice, and holds all three for my yes before
anything sends. I still approve every one. What I no longer do is write the first draft at
eleven at night, which is when every version of that email had been written until then.

Same product. Same day. A different first line. Zero lines of code changed, and the build was
never the weak part. Good products lose to better first lines every day, and the gap is not
talent, it is that nobody schedules the writing. It sits in the space between two calls because
it feels like the part that can be improvised, and it is the only part the customer actually
reads.
