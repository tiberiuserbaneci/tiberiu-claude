## CAPTION

I had four deals in flight last week and I only knew the status of one of them. That
is the honest part, because it is exactly what happens when you are the founder with no
sales operations function: the pipeline exists in your head, and your head is also the
place where product decisions, hiring conversations, and the investor update live. The
deal that needed attention was three rows down in a spreadsheet I had not opened in
two weeks. It was not dead. It was waiting. And waiting is the exact thing that turns
into not moving, which turns into not this quarter, which turns into not at all.

The problem is not that I do not care about the pipeline. The problem is that the
pipeline only works if someone updates it, and the founder is the only person who can,
and the founder does not have time.

- - -

What the week looked like, and what I would have missed without the system watching:

→ Monday: the pipeline updated from conversations, not from me. I had not opened the
spreadsheet. The system did, and it pulled the stage from the last email thread, the
last call note, and the last deliverable sent. The pipeline was current before I knew
it needed to be.

→ Wednesday: one deal had not moved in ten days. That is not a red flag to a founder
who is building a product and hiring a designer and answering support tickets. It is a
red flag to a system that counts days between stages, and it was the only thing that
made me reopen the spreadsheet.

→ Thursday: the follow-up drafted from the stage. Not a template. Not a sequence. The
system read the last conversation, found the natural next step, and wrote it in my own
words. I read it once, added one sentence about the call, and sent it. The deal moved
back to Proposal the next day.

→ Friday: the week-end summary arrived with what moved, what stalled, and what I close
next week. Four lines. Four minutes. And the only dashboard I opened all week.

- - -

That is the whole workflow: it watches, it flags, it drafts, and I decide. It does not
replace your CRM and it does not replace your judgement about which deals to prioritise.
It removes the gap between the conversation and the update, which is the exact gap that
kills deals at the stage where nobody is watching.
