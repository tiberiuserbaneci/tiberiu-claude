## CAPTION

On the second of August it became illegal for your AI to send a message without saying it is an
AI. Most founders read that as a provider problem. It is not.

I run outbound through Claude. I assumed the obligation sat with whoever built the model, the
way it sits with the manufacturer and not the driver. It does not. I read Article 50 properly,
then read the two American laws that were already live, and rewrote every sender line I use in
one afternoon.

- - -

Four things I had wrong:

→ Article 50 binds the deployer, not only the provider. You are the deployer of the AI that
sends your outbound, even when the tool belongs to someone else and you never touched a model.

→ The European ceiling is EUR 15M or 3% of worldwide turnover, and you will probably never meet
it. It is the number everyone quotes and the one least likely to reach a company your size.

→ California SB 243 is the one that actually reaches you. Enforceable since 1 January 2026,
with a private right of action: the person you wrote to can sue you directly at $1,000 a
violation, without waiting for a regulator to take an interest.

→ A label is not enough. Not "this is AI". The guidance asks who it works for, which means a
named company the reader can look up.

- - -

Here is the part I did not expect. Adding all of it did not cost me replies. It added them.

A message from nobody gets deleted. A message that says a machine wrote it, on behalf of a
company with a real name, gets read, because the reader stops trying to work out what they are
looking at. The compliance line and the trust line turned out to be the same line, and I had
been avoiding writing it for a year on the assumption that admitting the AI would kill the
response rate.

Every message I send now carries three lines: what wrote it, who it works for, and how to reach
a person instead. It took one afternoon to write them and they go at the foot of the message,
not the top, so they answer the question rather than opening with it. Nothing about that made
the outbound weaker, and one of the two American laws was already live while I was still
treating the whole thing as a European problem arriving in August.
