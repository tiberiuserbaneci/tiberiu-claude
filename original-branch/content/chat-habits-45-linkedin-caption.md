## CAPTION

The model was never the bottleneck.
The twelve tabs around Claude were.

- - -

For two years I ran GTM like a browser hostage. A tab for research, a tab for the CRM, a doc of prompts I kept rewriting and a notes file I pasted from every single morning. The models kept getting smarter and my output kept not moving. So I stopped shopping for intelligence, moved the whole operation into one chat box, and built eight habits around it instead of buying more tools.

- - -

Here is what actually changed.

→ One slash replaced four tools. /cortex researches the account, /specter writes the outbound, /striker runs the deal, /pulse writes the post. Or I type plain English and the router picks the agent and the model tier that the turn deserves.

→ A tier picker sits on every single turn. Lite for quick lookups, Smart for the real work, Deep when the judgement is hard. I stopped paying deep-model prices for zip-code questions, and I stopped giving hard pricing calls to the cheap model.

→ Memory loads in 5 layers before I type a word. Static instructions, business profile, integrations, the top 5 relevant memories and the date. I have not re-explained my ICP, my voice or my pipeline since.

→ Slow work became background jobs. Scrape 200 sites, enrich a list, transcribe a call. I keep chatting while it runs, and nothing reaches a buyer until I approve it.

→ Sharing became a link, not a screenshot. One session carries 44 pieces of proof: every turn, every source, every artifact. A screenshot ships exactly 1 of them. My cofounder opens the link, scrolls the whole machine, then forks the session and keeps working from turn 32.

- - -

The lesson was not a smarter model. A smarter model on its own changed almost nothing for me. The leverage was the distance between the model, the context and the tools, and for two years I kept those three in a dozen different places. Put them one keystroke apart and the chat box stops being a demo you show people and starts being where the work gets done.

Save this carousel, it is the eight-habit map of the whole surface.
